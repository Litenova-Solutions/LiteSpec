The system

One Model, Four Layers, Two Directions

Commitments flow down through Specify, Implement and Verify. Questions and world facts flow up into Intent; code and tests point up through marks. The model builder reads it all and computes what nobody may write by hand.

The system

One Tree Under One Product

Every record except the product has exactly one parent, so the book places each record once. Layers are transparent group nodes, never parents. Cross-layer links are connections, rendered as phrases.

Sixteen root types

goal, problem, topic, constraint, research, decision, actor, module, integration-event, role, permission, entry-point, external-system, runbook, exception, term. Their parent is the product, and no field is written.

Structural parents block at level G

Topic containment, feature solves, journey serves, scenario result, screen entryPoint, identifier containment. A missing one is record-parent-missing, for every record, drafts included.

Order decides placement

The first solves entry is the parent; reordering moves the feature in the book. Requirements sit by containment, not by list order.

Drafts and retired records

A draft parent is a present parent, so authors may write top-down or bottom-up. A retired record stands beside its replacement.

From Goal to Code, Before and After

The chain only works when every link is written. A large consumer's records, measured before the release, showed where links went missing; 3.0.0 writes each link as a record field with a check behind it.

Before: measured 2026-10-30

  • 19 of 925 requirements trace to a goal; 18 of 1,272 use cases do.
  • Journeys name the goal as a sentence, never as a link.
  • 112 of 112 journeys carry fulfills over 571 distinct requirements.
  • 11 use cases serve a whole journey instead of a step.

After: the 3.0.0 links

  • A journey's serves names exactly one goal record.
  • A requirement names its goal in cites, beside every other reason.
  • Only Behavior fulfills: journeys cite, and requirement-fulfillment-missing reports the rest.
  • Use cases, reactions, jobs and screens serve steps only, never the journey.

On migration, the 18 traced use cases stay traced; the rest report until Behavior serves their steps. The book places each record once, under its parent, with connections as phrases.

Check Levels, and the Boundary Each Check Guards

Every finding has a level. G reads records only and always blocks. S reads code through a stack reader and blocks where that reader is qualified. A is advisory. A missing structural parent is G for every record; other required links are A for tier 3 and G for tier 1.

Draft code never fails the build

A level S finding follows the record's own status: it blocks when the record itself is approved or reviewed, and reports while the record itself is a draft. An inherited draft shows as a label beside the finding.

Qualification is per reader

A finding takes the qualification of the reader that produced its facts. The .NET Roslyn reader is qualified; the golden corpus tests the reader bundle and does not gate a consumer.

The full list of 133 codes with level and meaning lives in docs/tools/model.md, matching apps/model/.../Finding.cs. Unknown codes fail the build.

Keeping it honest

The Book: The Model Made Readable

The book follows LiteSpec and adds nothing of its own: outlined by the record layers, labelled with LiteSpec terms, showing only computed judgements, and linking every entry to its file and line.

Outline

Records grouped under Intent, Behavior, Implementation and Proof, governance beside them. Each record once, under its parent. A record that cannot be placed is a finding, not a new root.

Views

The outline, the review queue (in model review order), the security queue, and ballots (tier 1 first, with recommendation and auto-approval date).

Record detail

Facts first: status, owner, risk tier, approval batch. Journeys as sentences with promises, consequences and open questions; use cases with results and Given/When/Then; aggregates as state cards.

The rail

"From goal to behavior": the trace up and down, then the marked code under Implementation and the tests under Proof.

Labels and hovers

Every label is a record type, field, status, glossary term or a link's reads-as phrase; hovering shows its glossary definition.

Offline and private

Loads from script shards with no server; reviewer verdicts stay in the browser and download as review.json.

Working with it

Who Wins: Authority, Precedence and Governance

Two different orders. Authority settles conflicts between records in a project. Precedence settles conflicts between pieces of LiteSpec guidance. Governance records let a project depart or accept a gap, visibly and with a date.

Authority between records

  1. constraint
  2. product
  3. requirement
  4. rule
  5. use case
  6. result
  7. scenario

Between peer decisions, the later approved one wins. Every other link is trace and decides nothing.

Precedence between guidance

  1. a project deviation
  2. a selected extension's replacement
  3. a deeper page
  4. a shallower page

If nothing decides two conflicting requirements: stop, quote both provision IDs, and ask.

One exception record

departure
covers a provision, subject, reason, expiresOn; applies once approved
gap
covers finding codes; reports without failing until expiresOn

Past expiresOn, exception-expired fails. An incident fix ships under a time-boxed exception that becomes a draft question within seven days.

Adoption: transcription mode

While adopting, litespec model backfill drafts Behavior from code, marked provenance: transcribed. Such a record stays draft until its Intent is approved. adoption.transcriptionEndsWith names the decision that ends the mode; then transcription-expired fails.

Code versus specification

The specification wins where Intent has spoken. Where it is silent, working code is evidence for a question: never a defect to delete, never a source to transcribe silently.

Working with it

Exceptions Replace Deviations and Gaps, Before and After

Two loose records with free-text links let debt hide. A large consumer's records showed how; 3.0.0 replaces both with one strict record.

Before: measured 2026-10-30

  • 105 unfulfilled requirements sit in known gaps, invisible in the open findings.
  • 5 of 22 deviations govern nothing: neither subject nor code.
  • A deviation once silenced approval-reopened on the record it names.

After: one exception record

  • A departure names its provision in covers and applies only once approved.
  • A gap names finding codes in covers and a record in subject; the findings report as excepted.
  • Every exception carries reason, expiresOn and approval; past the date, exception-expired fails.

Lantern's exception/flash-sale-evidence-later holds two findings on one requirement until 2027-02-01. An exception on a use case also covers its scenarios and results.

# Comparable Rationale Practice

A design rationale explains why a rule exists, what it costs, and what remains open. This research record compares how 5 comparable templates and processes keep that rationale. The 5 are Python's PEP 1, the Rust RFC template, the Kubernetes KEP template, MADR, and RFC 6855. LiteSpec uses the comparison to place its own rationale beside the rules it produces. A reader judges a rule harder without its reasons.

## Findings

Comparable projects keep rationale in numbered, dated records that are never edited, and they keep it beside the normative text (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). Each record states its motivation, its drawbacks, and its unresolved questions (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). The specification remains the only normative text (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`).

PEP 1 requires Rationale and Rejected Ideas sections (`peps-python-pep-0001`). It also names a draft's header as part of the proposal (`peps-python-pep-0001`). So the reasoning stays inside the proposal from its first draft (`peps-python-pep-0001`).

The Rust RFC template requires Motivation, Drawbacks, Rationale and Alternatives, and Unresolved Questions (`raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`). It states that a merged RFC is the shared-agreement text, not a final specification (`raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`).

The Kubernetes KEP template requires Risks and Mitigations, and Drawbacks (`raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`). It keeps each KEP as one directory of record beside the process (`raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`).

MADR 4.0.0, dated 17 September 2024, records the context, the options with their pros and cons, the chosen outcome, and the positive and negative consequences (`adr-github-madr`). It keeps all of them in one numbered file per decision (`adr-github-madr`).

RFC 6855, dated March 2013, keeps its design rationale in a non-normative appendix (`rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). The appendix lists the alternatives the RFC considered, and it stays separate from the protocol text (`rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`).

The pattern appears in all 5 sources (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). Rationale stands in numbered, dated records beside the normative text (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). Each record states its motivation, its drawbacks, and its unresolved questions (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). The specification remains the only normative surface (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). The record draws this pattern from the 5 sources (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`).

The record carries 2 limits (`peps-python-pep-0001`, `raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`, `raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`, `adr-github-madr`, `rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). ## Source Notes

The record read the 5 sources in full on 30 October 2026. They are PEP 1 (`peps-python-pep-0001`), the Rust RFC template (`raw-githubusercontent-rust-lang-rfcs-master`), the Kubernetes KEP template (`raw-githubusercontent-kubernetes-enhancements`), MADR (`adr-github-madr`), and RFC 6855 (`rfc-editor-rfc-rfc6855`). Each source carries a note on how the record read and used it.

The sources agree on the record shape. They disagree on where the rationale stands and whether the record is final. PEP, Rust, KEP, and MADR keep rationale inside the proposal or decision record. RFC 6855 moves it into a non-normative appendix. The record follows the majority and keeps its research and decision records beside its provisions, not inside them.

The record takes the Rust position that a merged RFC is shared agreement, not a final specification. It takes the MADR position of one file per decision, not the KEP position of one directory per proposal. All 5 sources require the author to state drawbacks or rejected alternatives. The record reads that requirement as evidence that rationale records discipline their authors.

Nygard's original ADR article is LiteSpec's existing citation in `docs/reference/decision-records.md`. The record did not list it as read. It states nothing about the practitioner commentary on stale decision records, because it did not read that commentary in full.

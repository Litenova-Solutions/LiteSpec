# Narrow the Writing Rules to Meaning

LiteSpec's writing rules make records easy to scan and easy to check. This decision applies each rule where it improves meaning and makes the rest advisory, so a rule never forces a writer to drop a condition or to state a fact as an action.

## Context

The actor-first rule made every sentence open with an actor, though a fact or a calculation reads better with its object as subject. "The statement closing date determines the comparison date" has no actor, and forcing one into it hides the fact.

The arriving-cold rule asked every record, section and page to open with who it concerns and what is at stake. That repeated the stake in each section of a record, so a reader met the same sentence several times.

The 25-word bound was required, and writers met it by dropping the conditions that a long sentence needed. The rationales presented the bounds as facts about readers and screens, when they were house limits chosen for scanning.

## Options

LiteSpec compared 3 options for the reach of the writing rules. The table sets each option against the same two points: what it gains and what it costs.

| Option | Gains | Costs |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Narrow each rule and make the length bound advisory | Facts read naturally, the stake appears once, and conditions survive. | A reviewer judges which sentences are actions, and a writer may ignore the advisory bound. |
| Keep every rule required | One rule applies to every sentence. | Facts are forced into actor form, the stake repeats, and conditions are dropped. |
| Drop the sentence bound | Conditions stay in place. | Long sentences reach a reader without any check. |

## Decision

The actor-first rule applies to a sentence that describes an action. A sentence that states a fact, a state or a calculation may take its object or its condition as subject. Passive voice that hides who acts is still a finding.

The arriving-cold rule applies to the opening paragraph of a record, an explanation file or a page. Each section answers its own question and does not repeat the opening.

The 25-word bound is advisory. A longer sentence that keeps a condition is not a defect, and where the bound and the rule to keep conditions pull apart, the condition wins. The eight-word title bound stays required, and a statement keeps its one-sentence rule with no modal word.

## Consequences

The 25-word limit on a glossary meaning becomes an advisory finding in place of a field error, so one bound has one strength. The rationales state the bounds as house limits. `use-literal-words` defines a loose word as one that leaves the act unclear in its sentence, so a field term used in its defined sense, such as an actor that holds a role, is not loose.

## Revisit Conditions

This decision holds until advisory bounds are ignored so often that the finding has no effect. It also holds until a reviewer cannot tell which sentences are actions, because the actor-first rule is applied to facts. Either report reopens the choice.
